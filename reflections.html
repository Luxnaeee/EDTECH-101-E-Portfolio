(0) The Seminar

The Seminar was a very interesting take on how we should conduct our classes in general and how beneficial it is to facilitate it all as learners.

From the start, I am very fond of how Khan described college, a place where we go not because of our professors or books but for the presence of another person. A place where same minds think alike which may end up creating harmonious discourses on different topics and opinions. The professors and books serve to become a starting point for interactions and discussions to take place. In my own experience, I believe this is true as I often have discussions with my peers on certain topics that were taught during class or from what I learned or heard somewhere. Everyday, I hear new things—new ideas and opinions on different topics—that are up for discussion. Through these discussions, you are able to learn more about certain topics and aspects you may not be familiar with. I agree with the line from the text that we already know things but we just don’t know it yet. I do believe that knowledge lies dormant within us and college helps us—the students, teachers, and even the staff—to nurture these knowledge in order to wield them in different conversations or discussions.

I found the proposed method of how we can help each other nurture the dormant knowledge within us interesting. Making use of a seminar style learning environment as a form of teaching in a classroom has never crossed my mind as a viable option. Yet, while reading this, the idea had become more appealing to me. Moving away from the idea of the traditional classroom—where the students are listening and learning from their teacher who is discussing and teaching—to a seminar presents an opportunity for everyone to equally pitch in their knowledge and opinions and learn from one another.

How great a seminar may sound, it is not without its problems. For one, I do agree that a seminar will not function as it should without the participation of the learners. Without their participation, there are no discussions, no exchanging of ideas or opinions thus no one learns anything. I do understand that people have a hard time to defend or explain their positions yet I do believe that this should not stop us from participating and learning from what others may say. Another problem is of course, the learners themselves. Which, to some extent, I do believe. I have encountered people that do not want to be corrected, to learn from others, or take in new ideas and would rather squeeze their own ideas until there is nothing left. A seminar is a place for discussions, for learning and sharing. If a person comes to a seminar that is not willing to learn from others and accept news ideas, then there’s no point for them to attend one in the first place. I do see that a seminar is a piece of art which is created by the participants. Each one has a share on how it is being done, what is being shared, and what are heard by those participating. It is meticulously crafted by the participants. Each part of the art was carefully laid-out for everyone to see, digest, and understand.

Though I still have some reservations for a seminar type of classroom, it definitely sounds more appealing and creates more opportunities for everyone to learn and share their own ideas than the traditional classrooms we are used to. I feel like an effective way to figure out if a seminar learning environment is effective would be if it would be used in different classroom situations. Only then will we truly understand and figure if the seminar truly presents the learners an opportunity to learn amongst themselves and for there to be an equal opportunity for learning.

(4) Instructional Media Resources:2D and 3D

Intructional media resources are different types of resources used by a person as a mode of instruction. These media resources, however, are not limited to classroom use and can be use by anyone in any situation as a tool for instruction.

Instructional media resources can be a 2D resource or a 3D resource. 2D instructional resources are Two-Dimensional objects that are used for instructions. These objects are usually flat on all sides. Examples of these are flashcards, flipcharts, whiteboard, blackboard, posters, and many more. On the other hand, 3D instructional resources are Three-Dimensional, meaning these objects usually have depth to them. Examples of 3D materials are models puppets, and even resource speakers.

As mentioned earlier, these instructional media resources are not limited to classroom use nor in subjetcts. Both 2D and 3D instructional materials are viable options to teach different arrays of topics or subject matters. For example, using flashcards you may use it to teach vocabulary, terms, or mathematical equations or creating models to visualize concepts. Moreover, 2D and 3D instructional media resources help promote the 4Cs carried by 21st century education as it challenges teachers and students a like to think critically, to be creative, collaborate, and foster communication with one another.

(6) Innovative Technologies for Assessment in Teaching and Learning

Assessment is an importan tool in teaching and the learning process at it helps both the students and teachers see if what they are doing are effective. For teachers, being assessed on their pedagogical approach is important as it tells them if it is effective or needs to be tweaked or changed completely. For the students, being assessed helps the teachers and parents understand if the students are actually learning something and are developing their skills and mindsets.

There are plenty of assessment tools a teacher, facilitator, or an instructor can utilize. Examples of these assessment tools are Plickers, Wordwall, Google Forms, Quizizz, HotPotatoes, Kahoot, Zipgrade, and Quizlet. Some of these assessment tools are free of charge and easy to navigate and use. However, some require for the user to subscribe in order to use the advance features they offer.

Quizizz, Quizlet, Kahoot, Plickers, Wordwall, and HotPotatoes are assessment tools that are more gamefied with Kahoot, QUizizz, Quizlet all having a leaderboard system. However, some of these requires for a moderator to be present in order for them to function. On the otherhand, Zipgrade and Google Forms are more of your old school assessment tools that are able to provide instant feedback and grades for the students to see.

Most of these assessment tools are online, meaning there is an automated process happening in the background. This is extremely useful for educators to easily assess their students in numbers and be graded quickly. Of course, the educator shoudld not rely solely on these tools and should still have a human input as these tools are not always accurate. Not only that, but for the students, it is a way for them to gain instant feedback on their work or understanding.

I believe that the Philippines are ready for these kinds of innovations, moreover, we are already using some of these in the classroom like Google Forms for more standarized tests and Quizizz and Kahoot for a more gamefied assessment session.

(8) Overall Course Journey

EDTECH 101 has been a really interesting subject and I can see and understand why it is a core subject. This course explores education in terms of classroom design. What should a classroom look like, what should a classroom environment be like for both students and teachers a like.

From the get go, the course and Teach Sam already makes you question what kind of classroom environment would you like to have once you become a teacher, what should be the things the educator as to consider for their classroom. With as starting to from the reading "The Seminar" which questions the traditional classroom setup, I personally have been asking myself, if not a traditional classroom, what and how should I design my classroom. Do I go about and try a seminar style of a classroom or a more radical idea.

The course itself also teaches you different tools for instruction and assessment and learing models you may apply once you finally have your own classroom. From 2D materials to Digitial ones, traditional assessments to digital assessments. Moreover, it teaches that no matter how technologically-driven the world is becoming, it won't hurt us to look back and use traditonal methods or tools inside of the classroom. Which just shows that a teacher should be critical, knowing what tools to use and when to use these tools appropriately.

EDTECH 101 also promotes a collaborative learning environment,one of the important skills one must learn in the 21st Century of education, one I wish I was able to put more effort into. Looking back, I was I involved myself more with my groupmates, though I may have helped, I know that I could've done more for the group and that's something I would like to improve upon more.

Overall, EDTECH 101 is an important core subject when it comes to taking up a degree in Education. Through this course, you are able to design your classroom with the necessary tools and assessments which can make the overal learning efficient and effective.

(9) Design Thinking: Wallet Challenge

The wallet challenge is an interesting way for 2 people to understand each other. Though it might seem simple, you are designing a wallet base on the interests and problems of the other person. Which makes the wallet very catered to them and one of a kind. This challenge tests your ability to communicate with another person, to know your capacity in building connections or relationships with others.

As instructional designer, it is very out-of-the-box. Not set of rules or requests in order to create such design. You really have to think, be critical of what the other person needs or wants to have. As an instructional designer, this not only challenges your ability to listen, but also to think critically and creatively in order to satisfy your partner.

(10) Course Reflection

Beyond the content of the course itself, the course brough valuable lessons that I can apply throughout my life. One is collaboration. As mentioned previously, EDTECH 101 promotes collaboration as most of the activities, tasks, and requirements of the course requires for you to collaborate with your groupmates. From crafting concept maps, to creating a lesson plan, you collaborate with the same 3 or 4 people. Moreover, this course also promotes communication and idea sharing. You are highly encouraged to communicate with your groupmates and share your ideas, discuss contrasting ideas, and come up with something better. Which leads to development of creativity and critical thinking. Which then covers all 4Cs a learner is expected to learn in 21st century education.

Moreover, the course taught me that I did not have to rush into anything and that everything truly is a process. The goal is to not accomplish everything all at once but to do them step by step and then compile everything into one final product.

EDTECH 101 not only made sure that I was able to design a classroom we want and lay out my processes into steps but it also helped me ensure that my classroom environment fosters growth, learning, understanding, patience, and warmth. All things I felt in class and by my professor.
