The Seminar

The Seminar was a very interesting take on how we should conduct our classes in general and how beneficial it is to facilitate it all as learners.

From the start, I am very fond of how Khan described college, a place where we go not because of our professors or books but for the presence of another person. A place where same minds think alike which may end up creating harmonious discourses on different topics and opinions. The professors and books serve to become a starting point for interactions and discussions to take place. In my own experience, I believe this is true as I often have discussions with my peers on certain topics that were taught during class or from what I learned or heard somewhere. Everyday, I hear new things—new ideas and opinions on different topics—that are up for discussion. Through these discussions, you are able to learn more about certain topics and aspects you may not be familiar with. I agree with the line from the text that we already know things but we just don’t know it yet. I do believe that knowledge lies dormant within us and college helps us—the students, teachers, and even the staff—to nurture these knowledge in order to wield them in different conversations or discussions.

I found the proposed method of how we can help each other nurture the dormant knowledge within us interesting. Making use of a seminar style learning environment as a form of teaching in a classroom has never crossed my mind as a viable option. Yet, while reading this, the idea had become more appealing to me. Moving away from the idea of the traditional classroom—where the students are listening and learning from their teacher who is discussing and teaching—to a seminar presents an opportunity for everyone to equally pitch in their knowledge and opinions and learn from one another.

How great a seminar may sound, it is not without its problems. For one, I do agree that a seminar will not function as it should without the participation of the learners. Without their participation, there are no discussions, no exchanging of ideas or opinions thus no one learns anything. I do understand that people have a hard time to defend or explain their positions yet I do believe that this should not stop us from participating and learning from what others may say. Another problem is of course, the learners themselves. Which, to some extent, I do believe. I have encountered people that do not want to be corrected, to learn from others, or take in new ideas and would rather squeeze their own ideas until there is nothing left. A seminar is a place for discussions, for learning and sharing. If a person comes to a seminar that is not willing to learn from others and accept news ideas, then there’s no point for them to attend one in the first place. I do see that a seminar is a piece of art which is created by the participants. Each one has a share on how it is being done, what is being shared, and what are heard by those participating. It is meticulously crafted by the participants. Each part of the art was carefully laid-out for everyone to see, digest, and understand.

Though I still have some reservations for a seminar type of classroom, it definitely sounds more appealing and creates more opportunities for everyone to learn and share their own ideas than the traditional classrooms we are used to. I feel like an effective way to figure out if a seminar learning environment is effective would be if it would be used in different classroom situations. Only then will we truly understand and figure if the seminar truly presents the learners an opportunity to learn amongst themselves and for there to be an equal opportunity for learning.
